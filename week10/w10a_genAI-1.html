2 Generative AI background

2.1 AI in bioinformatics and biology

While conventional machine learning (ML) approaches have been used for decades in bioinformatics and biology more broadly, two kinds of more complicated AI have been developed and applied more recently:

  • Deep learning / Neural nets have become commonly used in the past 10+ years
    • DeepMind’s AlphaFold – 3D protein structure prediction from sequence
    • DeepVariant – variant calling from sequencing data
    • Applications in image analysis, e.g. microscopy or histology images

    Also numerous applications outside of bioinformatics – random example: Assessing the efficacy of machine learning techniques to characterize soybean defoliation from unmanned aerial vehicles (Zhang et al. 2022)

  • Generative AI

2.2 Generative AI

Generative AI refers to a class of artificial intelligence models designed to create new content, such as text, images, music, or code. This has become extremely prominent since ChatGPT’s launch in 2022.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are a subset of generative AI that try to understand and can generate human language. They are trained on vast amounts of text data and can perform a variety of language-related tasks, including code writing.

The “P” in ChatGPT stands for “pre-trained”, meaning the model only knows about the data that it was trained on. However, some genAI models are also able to search the web, like openAI’s Deep Research. The usage of information outside of the training data is called “Retrieval Augmented Generation” (RAG), which also includes simpler cases where you upload a file and ask questions about it.

In simple terms, LLMs such as ChatGPT aim to predict the next part of a word or phrase based on a user-provided text prompt.

— https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.14325

However, it’s important to realize that LLMs don’t just go word-by-word: those kind of purely “sequential” models have been around for a long time and have long been present in Google when you type in your search phrase. LLMs use more complex architectures, such as “transformers”, which allow them to consider the broader context of the input text (the T in ChatGPT stands for “transformer”).

3 Ohio State genAI adoption

Ohio State University is one of several universities that have gone all-in on generative AI adoption in education.

Ohio State is leading a bold, groundbreaking initiative to integrate artificial intelligence into the undergraduate educational experience. The initiative will ensure that every Ohio State student, beginning with the class of 2029, will graduate being AI fluent — fluent in their field of study, and fluent in the application of AI in that field.

https://oaa.osu.edu/ai-fluency

This includes mandatory courses for first-year undergraduates in the use of genAI.

At Ohio State University in Columbus, students this year will take compulsory AI classes as part of an initiative to ensure that all of them are ‘AI fluent’ by the time they graduate.

But many education specialists are deeply concerned about the explosion of AI on campuses: they fear that AI tools are impeding learning because they’re so new that teachers and students are struggling to use them well. 4 Where to ask questions to genAI

4.1 Browser-based chat with Ohio State approved genAI tools

Ohio State currently has two main approved generative AI tools. This is important to know and use for two reasons:

  • You are able to use (more advanced models) without usage limits
  • You are complying with Ohio State’s data privacy and security policies. When you are logged using your OSU account, the data you provide to the AI tools is handled according to Ohio State’s policies. Therefore, you are to provide it with research data that you otherwise would not be allowed to share with third-party services.

These two tools are:

  • Microsoft Copilot
  • Google Gemini (since August 2025)

4.2 Inside IDEs

However, for coding tasks, it is often more convenient to use genAI directly inside your IDE, such as VS Code or RStudio.

Within IDEs, there can be two main modes of interaction, as we’ll see:

  • In-IDE chat

  • In-line autocomplete

  • Benefits of IDE-based assistance:

    • Automatic context awareness of your files
    • Less need to copy-paste code snippets
    • Easier to test and iterate

7 Tips and pitfalls

7.1 Advice on writing effective prompts

  • TRACI (Task, Role, Audience, Create, Intent)

  • Or:

    • Context - Who you are, what you’re working on, what the AI needs to know
    • Task - What specifically you want the AI to do
    • Format - How you want the output structured
    • Constraints - Any limitations, requirements, or things to avoid
    • Examples - Sample inputs/outputs if helpful

  • “Prompt enhancers”:

    • “Explain with examples” or “explain as if I’m a high school student”
    • “Please ask me clarifying questions”
    • “Tell me when you’re unsure about something”
    • Verify: “Show me the specific quotes from the papers that support each of these conclusions”

  • Be specific and detailed, e.g. about formats and programs

  • For bigger problems, break it down into smaller ones

7.2 Pitfalls

  • AI hallucinations

    • It doesn’t run code it suggests and may even misstate the expected output
    • May come up with functions/commands or columns that don’t exist

  • “0-to-90% problem”

  • “Jagged frontier”
