2.2 Generative AI

Since Chat GPT’s launch in 2022,

Generative AI refers to a class of artificial intelligence models designed to create new content, such as text, images, music, or code, based on patterns learned from existing data. These models use techniques like deep learning and neural networks to generate outputs that can mimic human creativity. Examples of generative AI include language models like GPT-4, which can produce coherent text, and image generation models like DALL-E, which can create images from textual descriptions. Generative AI has applications in various fields, including content creation, design, entertainment, and software development.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are a subset of generative AI that specialize in understanding and generating human language. They are trained on vast amounts of text data and can perform a variety of language-related tasks, including code writing. LLMs like OpenAI’s Codex and GPT-4 have been specifically designed to assist with programming by generating code snippets, debugging, and even writing entire functions based on natural language prompts. These models can understand programming languages and frameworks, making them valuable tools for developers looking to streamline their coding process and enhance productivity.

In simple terms, LLMs such as ChatGPT aim to predict the next part of a word or phrase based on a user-provided text prompt. https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.14325

8 Writing effective prompts

8.1 General advice

  • TRACI (Task, Role, Audience, Create, Intent)

  • Or:

    • Context - Who you are, what you’re working on, what the AI needs to know
    • Task - What specifically you want the AI to do
    • Format - How you want the output structured
    • Constraints - Any limitations, requirements, or things to avoid
    • Examples - Sample inputs/outputs if helpful

  • “Prompt enhancers”:

    • “Explain with examples” or “explain as if I’m a high school student”
    • “Please ask me clarifying questions”
    • “Tell me when you’re unsure about something”
    • Verify: “Show me the specific quotes from the papers that support each of these conclusions”

  • Be specific and detailed, e.g. about formats and programs

  • For bigger problems, break it down into smaller ones

9 Pitfalls

  • AI hallucinations

    • It doesn’t run code it suggests and may even misstate the expected output
    • May come up with functions/commands or columns that don’t exist

  • 0-to-90% problem

  • “Jagged frontier”

  • “How many n’s are in the word unconventional?”

  • “How many b’s in blueberry”
